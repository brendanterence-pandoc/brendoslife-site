Tailored Colonial

STYLE GUIDE v4  •  6506 WATERS EDGE WAY, LAKEWOOD RANCH  •  BRENDAN O’CONNOR  •  AUGUST 2026  •  BRENDOSLIFE.COM
British Colonial foundation. Traditional silhouettes with the ornament simplified. Blue as the ground color, white in the light layer, dark wood as the structure, contemporary lighting overhead. A tailored blend of classic American and Florida plantation influences — traditional, but modern.

The One Rule

Dark and structural at the furniture level; light at the wall and textile level. Every selection is evaluated against this principle. Case goods and primary seating are dark and substantial, and the surfaces around them are white, ivory, or pale blue. Applied consistently, each room reads confident and composed. If a room begins to feel heavy, the visual weight has moved too high and should be rebalanced. The house should feel equally comfortable and welcoming to Marcela, to guests, and to the dog.

The House We Actually Have

The home is a rental. The walls are a warm tan and will likely remain so; the owner is hesitant about repainting, and while I will ask, the plan cannot depend on it. White therefore enters through the furnishings: large pale rugs to ground each room, ivory upholstery, linen panels mounted high and wide, art in wide white mats, and mirrors where appropriate. Tan walls are favorable to dark wood and blue, so the scheme holds — the light layer simply needs greater presence.

Two additional conditions. The floors are tan tile throughout, so rugs carry more of the composition than they would over wood. And nothing will be built in; every piece must transition to a future home.

One helpful existing condition: the fixtures already in place are black — the drum chandelier in the front room and the fan in the primary bedroom — which supports blackened steel as the house metal.

Furniture

In

  • Low or high priced. What earns a place is value, story, sentimentality, relationship, history
  • High quality, preferably at a reasonable price
  • No full retail anywhere
  • English-arm and track-arm sofas, tailored, tight or slipcovered
  • Dark casegoods in mahogany, walnut, espresso. Clean faces, honest legs
  • Panel bed or four-poster. Posts yes, canopy no
  • Antiques, restored or reupholstered as needed, provided they work for a living. No museum pieces
  • The recliner. Saddle or chestnut leather with solid sides — a firm requirement, so the dog can sit alongside
  • Cane in restrained doses — a cane-back chair or two, each approved individually
  • Slatted wood screens and open shelving dividers for zoning the office without permanent construction
  • Jute and sisal underfoot. Woven structure on the lanai
  • White-painted or ivory pieces to balance the dark wood

Out

  • Anything fake. No reproductions posing as antiques, no faux finishes, no resin pretending to be wood
  • Nothing included because it is expensive only
  • No matching sets. Pieces should be selected individually and thoughtfully
  • Full rattan suites
  • Carving, applied molding, pineapple finials, tropical kitsch
  • Overstuffed club pieces, deep tufting, nailheads on everything
  • Oxblood chesterfields and expanses of dark leather
  • Palm-print upholstery and theme-restaurant nautical
  • Heavy, dark, club-style treatments

Nautical, Done Right

I fish and ran boats for years, so authentic maritime pieces belong in this home — used with restraint, one or two genuine vintage pieces per room, never as a theme. The precedent is a previous home where a pair of antique teal oars on one wall made the entire statement. The rowboat painting qualifies. The spotting scope on its wooden tripod qualifies. The weathered lifeguard chair qualifies, placed poolside. Catalog nautical décor — rope-wrapped mirrors and the like — does not. Authenticity and restraint are the standard.

Textiles

Linen and cotton in white, ivory, and pale blue, run a shade paler than usual while the walls remain tan. Stripes, quiet block prints, small-scale geometry. No florals, no chintz, no palm fronds. Performance fabric on high-use seating, with the dog in mind. Drapery is simple linen, mounted high and wide, no swags. Rugs run large — light-grounded with blue pattern, or jute layered under wool. Over this much tan tile, the rug defines the floor plane.

Lighting and Art

Lighting is the contemporary counterpoint. Clean fixtures in blackened steel or bronze, linen drum or paper shades, contemporary lamps on traditional wood. No faux-candle chandeliers. No fan-with-light-kit in the main rooms. Fans on the lanai for climate comfort.

Art is contemporary or genuinely old, and always authentic. The budget favors pieces around five thousand dollars, stretching toward twenty thousand for work that holds its value — value retention is a safeguard, not the objective; I buy art to live with it. Editions, photography, good ceramics, established artists. A signed edition from a respected artist is preferred over a familiar reproduction, and one large, confident piece is preferred over several small ones. Wide white mats and light frames, which also counter the tan walls. I purchase the art directly, with Marcela’s input. The designer advises on scale and placement only, with no acquisition commission.

What I Already Own

The photo review sorted existing pieces into two groups. For the main living areas: the rowboat painting, the three Audubon bird prints, the spotting scope, the crystal, and the countertop espresso machine. The teal velvet headboard is more restrained than it sounds and is a candidate for the guest room.

The personal collection — the signed Le Mans panoramic, the carbon Porsche hood, the Martini poster, the Raikkonen photo, the Masters flag, the diplomas, and the Steelers pieces — will be displayed together in the fourth bedroom, which becomes the office. This keeps the main rooms tailored while giving the collection a dedicated space.

The red retro refrigerator remains in the garage or moves to Donovan’s room.

Who Decides

I make final decisions. Marcela contributes throughout and holds meaningful input on the shared spaces. The designer is the professional third voice. When the three of us agree, we proceed; when we do not, we pause, and I decide.

Paste-Ready Designer Brief

"British colonial bones. Traditional silhouettes with the ornament stripped out. Dark wood furniture, blue as the ground color, light tailored textiles in linen and crisp stripes, no florals or tropical prints. The house is a rental with tan walls that will likely remain, so whites carry the light through large pale rugs, high-mounted drapery, ivory upholstery, and white-matted art. Contemporary lighting in blackened steel. Contemporary and authentic art as the counterpoint, owner-purchased. Functional antiques welcome. Restrained cane case by case, no full rattan, nothing fake, no matching sets. Authentic maritime pieces in small doses, never a theme. Slatted screens for zoning. Tailored, not heavy. Dark and structural at the furniture level, light at the textile level. Ralph Lauren crossed with Florida plantation."
